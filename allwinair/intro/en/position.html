The starting point: agencies could not sell ancillaries

Most airline tickets are sold by travel agencies: about 80% for full-service carriers, about 70% for low-cost carriers and about 90% for foreign airlines. Yet until 2019, travelers could not buy seat selection or extra baggage on agency sites; they had to buy them separately from the airline or at the airport.

Why this position matters now

  • Ancillaries are a growing share of airline revenue; leading low-cost carriers earn over 40% of revenue from ancillaries.
  • Sellers struggle to make money on tickets alone; ancillaries are a new revenue stream.
  • In B2B gateways, whoever builds the connections first becomes the standard. ALLWIN AIR is already connected with 7 Korean airlines and 13 agencies and OTAs.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AGS (Ancillary Gateway System)?
A B2B unified sales system that connects many airlines and ancillary suppliers with many sellers to distribute ancillaries — the ancillary counterpart of a GDS for tickets. ALLWIN AIR operates it.
Does ALLWIN AIR compete with GDSs?
No. A GDS connects ticket booking and issuing; ALLWIN AIR connects the ancillary sales that follow. Sellers keep their ticket flow and add ALLWIN AIR on top.
Why couldn't agencies sell ancillaries before?
Each airline has different systems and rules for ancillaries, so agencies had to integrate and operate each airline separately. ALLWIN AIR does that integration and operation in one place.
